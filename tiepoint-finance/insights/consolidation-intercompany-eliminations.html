Multi-Entity Consolidation and Intercompany Eliminations

How eliminations work, how to match and resolve intercompany differences, and how to prove the consolidated numbers are right, with a fully worked example.

Key takeaways

  • Both IFRS 10 and ASC 810 require intragroup balances and transactions to be eliminated in full, including profit still sitting in group assets such as inventory.
  • Most consolidation pain is not the elimination entry. It is matching: getting both sides of each intercompany relationship to agree before close.
  • Every intercompany difference is one of four things: timing, FX, classification or a genuine dispute. Classify before you fix.
  • Eliminations with offsetting P&L lines (management fees, interest) do not change group profit. Unrealized profit does, and it is the elimination most often missed.
  • Check how your tool treats partly owned entities. Proportional inclusion is not the same as full consolidation with a non-controlling interest.
  • The release test is simple: after eliminations, intercompany nets to 0.00 at group level for every period.

Who this is for: group controllers, consolidation accountants, FP&A leaders, finance teams in global capability centers and shared service centers who prepare or review consolidated management reporting across several legal entities, and finance systems managers configuring consolidation in an ERP or FP&A platform.

What consolidation is trying to achieve

Consolidated statements present a parent and its subsidiaries as a single economic entity. A group cannot sell to itself, lend to itself or earn a fee from itself, so anything that happened between group entities must be removed.

The standards say this directly. IFRS 10 (paragraph B86(c)) requires an entity to "eliminate in full intragroup assets and liabilities, equity, income, expenses and cash flows" and to eliminate in full profits or losses resulting from intragroup transactions that are recognized in assets. Under US GAAP, ASC 810-10-45-1 states that "intra-entity balances and transactions shall be eliminated", and that any intra-entity profit or loss on assets remaining within the group shall be eliminated.

The consolidation sequence

Order matters. Running steps out of sequence creates differences that look like errors but are really timing within your own process.

  1. Local close. Each entity closes its own books, including revaluation of foreign-currency monetary balances.
  2. Intercompany confirmation. Entities agree their intercompany balances and transactions with each counterparty (ideally on a cut-off date a few days before the main close).
  3. Translation. Each entity's results are translated into the presentation currency under the group's FX policy (see our guide on foreign currency translation).
  4. Aggregation. Translated results are added together on the group chart of accounts.
  5. Eliminations. Intercompany balances, transactions, unrealized profit, and investment against equity are eliminated.
  6. Non-controlling interest. The share of partly owned subsidiaries' net assets and profit that belongs to outside shareholders is calculated and presented.
  7. Top-side adjustments. Group-level entries that do not belong in any single entity's ledger, kept in a separate, labeled layer.
  8. Review and tie-out. The Five Tie-out Checks, including the intercompany zero-check.

ERPs that consolidate natively enforce a similar sequence. In NetSuite, for example, the Eliminate Intercompany Transactions task is the last task in the Period Close Checklist and can be completed only after the Revalue Open Foreign Currency Balances and Calculate Consolidated Balances tasks.

The elimination types

Each elimination below is shown as a template journal. "IC" means an intercompany account on the group chart of accounts.

1. Receivables and payables

What: one entity's receivable from another group entity, and that entity's matching payable.

Debit Credit
IC payable (buyer) X
IC receivable (seller) X

No effect on profit. The risk is in the match, covered below.

2. Intercompany revenue and cost

What: sales of goods or services between group entities.

Debit Credit
IC revenue (seller) X
IC cost of sales or expense (buyer) X

If the goods have all been sold on to external customers by period end, this entry is complete and has no effect on group profit. If some are still in the buyer's inventory, see unrealized profit below.

3. Loans and interest

What: a group entity lends to another.

Debit Credit
IC loan payable (borrower) X
IC loan receivable (lender) X
IC interest income (lender) Y
IC interest expense (borrower) Y

Accrued interest receivable and payable are eliminated the same way. Watch for loans denominated in a currency other than one party's functional currency: the revaluation gains and losses each side records will not offset neatly, and the residual needs a defined home under your FX policy.

4. Management fees, recharges and service income

What: fees for shared services, cost recharges and cost-plus service arrangements (common for captive service centers and global business services organizations).

Debit Credit
IC fee or service income (provider) X
IC fee or service expense (recipient) X

No effect on group profit, but significant effect on entity-level profit, which is why transfer pricing policy matters for the entities even though it disappears in consolidation.

5. Dividends

Dividends paid by a subsidiary to its parent are eliminated against the parent's dividend income.

6. Investment in subsidiary against equity (high level)

The parent's investment in a subsidiary is eliminated against the subsidiary's equity at acquisition, with goodwill and any non-controlling interest recognized. This is usually set up once at acquisition and carried forward, and it is typically owned by the group accounting team rather than generated monthly from transactions. We mention it here so that the consolidated balance sheet tie-out accounts for it.

7. Unrealized profit in inventory (high level)

What: goods sold between group entities at a margin and still held by the buyer at period end. From the group's point of view, that margin has not been earned yet.

Debit Credit
Cost of sales (group) U
Inventory (buyer) U

where U = unsold intercompany inventory × seller's margin percentage.

The entry reverses in the following period as the goods are sold externally. A tax effect may also arise, and PwC's guidance notes that intercompany profit is eliminated from the asset on a before-tax basis with tax effects considered separately. Agree the treatment with your group accounting and tax advisers.

Matching and mismatch resolution

Build the matching keys first

Matching works only if every intercompany line carries:

  • the counterparty (the IC partner dimension),
  • the intercompany account type (receivable, revenue, loan and so on, mapped to dedicated group accounts),
  • a reference both sides can see (invoice number, loan agreement, recharge schedule),
  • the transaction currency and amount, as well as each side's functional currency amount.

Without the counterparty on the line, you can only compare totals, and differences between three or more entities become impossible to untangle.

Classify every difference before fixing it

Category Example Typical resolution
Timing Seller invoiced on 30 June; buyer booked on 2 July. Goods or cash in transit. Under a common policy, the receiving side accrues or records the in-transit item. At group level, a top-side entry to cash or inventory in transit.
FX Each side translated a USD-denominated balance at different rates, or one side revalued and the other did not. Agree the transaction-currency amount first. The residual after translation goes to the account your FX policy specifies.
Classification Buyer booked the charge to an external supplier account or a non-IC expense line. Reclassify at source so that next month matches automatically.
Dispute Buyer disagrees with the amount invoiced. Escalate to the owners named in the intercompany policy; agree before close or record per policy.

Rules that prevent most differences

  • An intercompany cut-off date a few days before close, after which no new IC invoices are raised for the period.
  • One side leads. A common policy is that the seller's invoice is authoritative and the buyer books to it.
  • Transaction currency agreement. Both sides agree the amount in the transaction currency, not in their own functional currencies.
  • A tolerance for process, not for results. A small tolerance can decide which differences are investigated before close. The consolidated intercompany balance after eliminations must still be 0.00, with any residual explicitly booked and explained.

Partly owned subsidiaries and non-controlling interest (high level)

When the parent controls a subsidiary but owns less than 100%, the subsidiary is still consolidated in full (100% of its assets, liabilities, income and expenses), and the outside shareholders' share is presented as non-controlling interest (NCI) in equity, with profit attributed between the parent and the NCI.

Two practical points:

  1. Elimination of unrealized profit is in full regardless of NCI. ASC 810-10-45-18 states that the amount of intra-entity income to be eliminated "is not affected by the existence of a noncontrolling interest". The guidance permits the elimination to be allocated between the parent and NCI. PwC's guide explains that when the parent sells to a partly owned subsidiary (downstream), the elimination is usually attributed entirely to the parent, while for sales from a partly owned subsidiary to the parent (upstream), either full attribution to the parent or proportionate allocation between the parent and NCI is acceptable. Confirm your policy with your auditors.
  2. Check what your tool means by "consolidation percentage". Business Central's consolidation, for example, includes the stated percentage of each business unit: its documentation's example says that if you own 70% of a company, consolidation includes $70 of a $100 invoice. That is proportional inclusion, which is not the same as full consolidation with NCI presented separately. Some FP&A platforms handle NCI explicitly (Planful's consolidation process, for instance, calculates non-controlling interest, and Workday's Adaptive Planning consolidation uses a consolidation percentage sheet to choose methods and track minority interest). Whatever the tool, test its output against a hand calculation for one partly owned entity before you rely on it.

Worked example

Illustrative example. All entities, amounts and percentages are made up. One currency (USD) is used throughout to keep the focus on eliminations; our FX guide covers translation.

The group

  • Parent (P): owns 100% of Sub A and 80% of Sub B.
  • Sub A (A): distributor. Buys goods from P.
  • Sub B (B): service business. Pays P a management fee and has borrowed from P.

Intercompany activity in the period

  1. P sold goods to A for 1,000. P's cost was 700, so P's margin is 30% of the selling price. By period end A had sold 60% of these goods externally; 400 (at transfer price) is still in A's inventory. A has paid 600 and owes P 400.
  2. P charged B a management fee of 200. B has paid 50, which P has not yet received (cash in transit at period end). So P shows a receivable of 200; B shows a payable of 150.
  3. P lent B 5,000. Interest for the period is 50, accrued and unpaid on both sides.

Step 6: The intercompany zero-check

After T1 and E1 to E8, every intercompany account (receivables, payables, loans, interest, IC revenue, IC cost, fee income and expense) sums to 0.00 at group level. That is Check 3 of the Five Tie-out Checks, and it is the test we require to pass before any consolidated report is released.

Common failure modes in consolidation

Failure mode Symptom Detection test
Missing partner IC balances that cannot be matched to a counterparty Every IC group account requires a partner value; count lines without one
One-sided booking Difference equals a single invoice amount Pair test by reference number
Forgotten margin Group gross margin overstated in periods of IC inventory build-up Report IC inventory held at period end, by seller, every month
Double elimination Group revenue understated by an IC amount ERP elimination entries and platform elimination rules both active; compare elimination totals to IC totals
Proportional by accident Partly owned entity appears at less than 100% with no NCI line Hand-calculate one partly owned entity and compare
FX residual parked in IC Small, persistent IC differences that move with exchange rates Agree IC in transaction currency; review residual account monthly
