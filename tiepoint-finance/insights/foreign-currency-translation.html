Foreign Currency Translation for Consolidated Reporting

Functional versus presentation currency, the current-rate method under ASC 830 and IAS 21, where the cumulative translation adjustment comes from, and how to govern rates so your FP&A platform matches your books.

Key takeaways

  • Translation starts with a judgment, not a rate: each entity's functional currency (the currency of its primary economic environment). Everything else follows from it.
  • Under the current-rate method, assets and liabilities use the closing rate, income and expenses use transaction-date rates (an average is a permitted approximation when rates are stable), and equity is carried at historical rates. The difference goes to the cumulative translation adjustment (CTA) in other comprehensive income.
  • The CTA is not a plug. You can recompute it independently, and you should, every period.
  • Translate each month at that month's rates and accumulate. Retranslating year-to-date figures at a year-to-date average gives a different answer.
  • Most FX differences between an FP&A platform and the ERP come from rate governance, not from the accounting: a different source, a different average method, or a different rate type on an account.

Who this is for: group controllers, FP&A leaders and finance systems managers consolidating entities with different functional currencies, and anyone who has watched a consolidated balance sheet refuse to balance by an amount that moves with exchange rates.

Two currencies, two questions

Functional currency is, in IAS 21's words, "the currency of the primary economic environment in which the entity operates." ASC 830 uses the same concept. Determining it is an entity-by-entity judgment. IAS 21 (paragraph 9) points first to the currency that mainly influences sales prices for goods and services, and the currency that mainly influences labor, material and other costs. ASC 830 sets out a list of economic indicators covering similar ground (cash flows, sales prices and markets, expenses, financing and intercompany activity).

Presentation currency (IAS 21) or reporting currency (ASC 830) is the currency the consolidated statements are presented in. IAS 21 permits an entity to present its financial statements in any currency.

A practical consequence for systems: an entity's ledger currency is not necessarily its functional currency. If a captive service center keeps its books in its local currency but its functional currency were determined to be USD (an unusual conclusion, but possible), its results would first be remeasured into USD before any translation. The configuration of the ERP and the FP&A platform must reflect the accounting conclusion, not the other way round.

Translation versus remeasurement (high level)

Remeasurement Translation
Purpose Restate amounts into the entity's functional currency, as if the books had been kept in it Convert functional-currency statements into the group's presentation or reporting currency
When Foreign-currency transactions and balances; books kept in a currency other than the functional currency Consolidating an entity whose functional currency differs from the presentation currency
Monetary items Closing rate Closing rate
Non-monetary items at historical cost Historical rate Closing rate (as part of all assets and liabilities)
Where differences go Profit or loss (net income) Other comprehensive income (CTA)

At the transaction level, IAS 21 (paragraph 23) requires foreign-currency monetary items to be translated at the closing rate, and non-monetary items measured at historical cost at the exchange rate at the date of the transaction. Under ASC 830, where a foreign entity's books are not kept in its functional currency, PwC's guide (citing ASC 830-10-45-17) describes a two-step process: remeasure into the functional currency first, then translate into the reporting currency.

The rest of this guide is about translation.

The current-rate method: rates by line

Item IAS 21 ASC 830 (per PwC Viewpoint)
Assets and liabilities Closing rate at the date of each statement of financial position (para 39(a)) Exchange rate at the end of the reporting period
Income and expenses Exchange rates at the dates of the transactions (para 39(b)); an average rate for the period is often used as an approximation, but is inappropriate if rates fluctuate significantly (para 40) Rate on the date the income or expense is recognized; a weighted-average rate for the period is generally appropriate
Share capital and other contributed equity Not specified line by line; historical rates are standard practice Historical rates on the dates the equity entries were recorded
Retained earnings Built from translated profit each period Opening balance at historical rates; current-period change at the rates used for that period's income statement
Resulting differences Recognized in OCI and accumulated in a separate component of equity (paras 39(c), 41) Cumulative translation adjustment in accumulated OCI

Further points that matter in practice:

  • NCI share of the CTA. IAS 21 paragraph 41 requires accumulated exchange differences attributable to non-controlling interests to be allocated to them.
  • Goodwill and fair value adjustments. IAS 21 paragraph 47 treats goodwill and fair value adjustments arising on the acquisition of a foreign operation as assets and liabilities of that foreign operation, so they are translated at the closing rate. PwC's ASC 830 guidance similarly describes pushing parent-level accounts relating to a foreign entity (goodwill, intangibles, certain reserves and adjustments) down to that entity so they are treated consistently.
  • Disposal. On disposal of a foreign operation, IAS 21 paragraph 48 requires the cumulative exchange differences relating to it to be reclassified from equity to profit or loss. Under ASC 830, translation adjustments are released to net income on sale or substantially complete liquidation of the foreign entity.

Where the CTA comes from, and how to recompute it

Translation applies one rate to the balance sheet and different rates to the P&L and equity. The translated balance sheet therefore does not balance by itself, and the difference is the CTA. Its movement in a period has three main sources:

  1. Opening net assets × (closing rate − opening rate). The entity's net assets at the start of the period were translated at last period's closing rate; they are now translated at this period's closing rate.
  2. Net income × (closing rate − rate used for the P&L). Profit was translated at average (or transaction) rates but sits in net assets at the closing rate.
  3. Equity transactions during the period × (closing rate − rate on the transaction date). Capital injections, dividends and similar items.

PwC's illustrative example in its foreign currency guide decomposes the CTA in exactly this way. This decomposition is the most useful independent test in FX reporting: if the CTA your platform produces does not equal your recomputation, some line is being translated at the wrong rate or the wrong rate type.

Equivalently: net assets at closing rate are 700 × 1.30 = 910; equity at historical and translated amounts is 560 + 230 + 125 = 915; CTA balance = 910 − 915 = (5).

Step 3: Recompute the CTA independently

Opening CTA: opening net assets were 600 GBP (share capital 400 + opening retained earnings 200). At the opening closing rate that is 600 × 1.20 = 720 USD, against equity of 560 + 230 = 790 USD. Opening CTA balance = (70).

Movement in Year 2:

Source Calculation USD
Opening net assets 600 × (1.30 − 1.20) 60
Net income 100 × (1.30 − 1.25) 5
Equity transactions none in the year 0
Total movement 65

Closing CTA = opening (70) + movement 65 = (5). This agrees with Step 2. The movement of 65 is what appears in other comprehensive income for the year. If the platform showed anything else, we would look for an account translated at the wrong rate type.

Monthly translation in FP&A platforms

Management reporting runs monthly, and that raises a design decision accounting standards leave to policy.

  • Translate each month's activity at that month's average rate, then accumulate. The year-to-date P&L in USD is the sum of twelve translated months. This matches how most ERPs translate period activity.
  • Do not retranslate year-to-date local-currency totals at a year-to-date average unless your policy explicitly says so. It produces a different number, and the difference reappears as an unexplained CTA movement.
  • Know how your "average" is calculated. NetSuite's consolidated Average rate, for example, is a weighted average based on transaction amounts in the period, not a simple average of daily rates. Business Central uses an "Average Rate (Manual)" method for income statement accounts, entered by the user. An FP&A platform that computes its own simple average from a market feed will not tie to either.
  • Confirm the rate type on every account. NetSuite assigns each account a General Rate Type: Current for balance sheet accounts other than equity, Average for income statement accounts, Historical for equity. Business Central's Consol. Translation Method offers Average Rate (Manual), Closing Rate, Historical Rate, Composite Rate (typically for retained earnings) and Equity Rate. Mapping the right method to each group account is part of the chart of accounts design, not an afterthought.
  • Prefer importing the ERP's rates. Cube, for example, documents an option to apply NetSuite's consolidated exchange rates by account during import, with income statement accounts defaulting to average and balance sheet accounts to current. Using the ledger's own rates is the simplest way to make Check 4 of the Five Tie-out Checks pass.

Rate sourcing and governance

An FX policy for reporting should answer, in writing:

Question Typical decision points
Source Which provider or central bank; one source for all currency pairs
Fixing time Which daily fixing or cut-off (for example, a specified time and market)
Closing rate Rate on the last business day of the period, or the period-end calendar date
Average rate Simple average of daily rates, average of month-end rates, or transaction-weighted (as computed by the ERP)
Historical rates How equity rates are recorded and maintained, and who keeps the register
Approval Who approves the period's rate table, and when it is locked
Changes What happens if a rate is corrected after lock: re-run, or adjust prospectively
Significant fluctuation When an average is no longer appropriate (IAS 21 para 40), and what to use instead

Controls we recommend:

  1. One approved rate table per period, locked before consolidation.
  2. A comparison of the FP&A platform's rate table to the ERP's, every period, for every currency pair and rate type.
  3. A register of historical equity rates, reconciled to the equity accounts.
  4. The CTA recomputation above, for each foreign operation, every period.
  5. A documented treatment for any residual translation rounding.

Special situations to flag early

  • Highly inflationary economies. ASC 830-10-45-11 describes a highly inflationary economy as one with cumulative inflation of approximately 100 percent or more over a 3-year period. Under US GAAP, a foreign entity in such an economy is remeasured as if its functional currency were the reporting currency. Under IFRS, IAS 29 applies. These cases need specific accounting advice and a different system configuration.
  • Lack of exchangeability. The IASB's amendments to IAS 21 (Lack of Exchangeability, issued August 2023) set out how to assess whether a currency is exchangeable and which rate to use when it is not. They apply for annual reporting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2025.
  • Intercompany loans that are part of the net investment. Exchange differences on monetary items that form part of a net investment in a foreign operation can follow a different path in the consolidated statements. Identify these loans explicitly in the intercompany register.
  • Change of functional currency. If an entity's circumstances change, the change is applied prospectively. Plan the system cut-over with your accountants.

Checklist: FX readiness for a consolidation build

  • Functional currency documented for every entity, with the reasoning
  • Presentation or reporting currency confirmed
  • Ledger currency versus functional currency confirmed (remeasurement needed?)
  • Rate source, fixing time and average method written into the FX policy
  • Rate type assigned to every group account (closing, average, historical, composite)
  • Historical equity rates register in place
  • Monthly translation approach agreed (month by month, then accumulate)
  • Platform rate table reconciled to ERP rate table for every period in scope
  • CTA recomputed independently for each foreign operation
  • Net-investment loans, hyperinflationary entities and unusual currencies flagged
