The TiePoint Method

Four gates. Evidence at every one.

A finance systems project should never ask you to trust it. Each phase below ends with named deliverables, the working artifacts behind them, and the evidence you review before you sign off.

The Five Tie-out Checks

Why each check exists, and what it catches.

A report can look right and still be wrong. These five checks target the specific ways finance data goes wrong between source system and dashboard.

CheckWhy it matters and what it catchesPass condition
  1. 01

    Every GL account is mapped exactly once

    Reports only add up if each ledger account lands in one reporting line, once.

    CatchesNew accounts opened after go-live that silently drop out of totals, and accounts assigned to two lines that double count.

    unmapped = 0
  2. 02

    Totals tie to the trial balance

    The trial balance is the agreed source of truth, so every entity and every month is compared against it.

    CatchesPartial or failed loads, cut-off timing differences, and late journals that were posted but never refreshed.

    variance = 0.00
  3. 03

    Intercompany nets to zero

    A group should not report trading with itself. After eliminations, intercompany balances must cancel.

    CatchesOne-sided postings, mismatched counterparty codes, and FX differences left on intercompany balances.

    IC net = 0.00
  4. 04

    Correct FX rates and periods

    Translation uses different rate types for different balances, and each month needs its own rates.

    CatchesThe wrong rate type, a rate from the wrong month, a missing currency, or a translation difference that was never booked.

    rates = policy
  5. 05

    Large movements are explained

    A number can tie to the ledger and still mislead. Big swings need a reason before a board sees them.

    CatchesMisclassified postings, reversals in the wrong period, one-off items, and mapping changes that moved history.

    unexplained = 0
Countermeasures

The pitfalls the method is built to avoid.

Every gate exists because of a failure we would rather prevent than explain.

Building dashboards before the data ties

It feels like progress, but every later fix changes the numbers people already saw. We prove the data first.

Mapping in a spreadsheet nobody owns

A mapping table without an owner drifts within a quarter. We assign ownership and a rule for new accounts.

Treating consolidation as a report

Eliminations and FX translation are accounting logic. We agree them with your controller before they are built.

Loading only the current month

One clean month proves little. We reconcile several recent closed months to catch timing and cut-off issues.

Skipping the runbook

When the person who built it leaves, so does the knowledge. Handover includes written steps and training.

Over-broad system access

Admin access is rarely needed. We work read-only wherever possible and remove access at the end.

Discovery questionnaire

The questions we ask in discovery.

Useful even if you never hire us: answering these honestly tells you how big your project really is. Print it or save it as a PDF from your browser.

TiePoint Finance · Discovery questionnaire

23 questions in 6 groups

Send us your answers

01Systems

  1. Which ERP or accounting systems hold your general ledger, and which versions or editions?
  2. Which other sources matter for reporting: CRM, HRIS, payroll, banks, billing?
  3. Which FP&A or BI tool do you use today, or which are you evaluating?
  4. How does data move between these systems now: connectors, exports, manual entry?

02Structure

  1. How many legal entities do you report on, and in which countries?
  2. What is each entity's functional currency, and what is your presentation currency?
  3. Is there intercompany trading, lending or cost recharging between entities?
  4. Do you use dimensions such as department, class, location or project? Are they consistent across entities?

03Reporting

  1. Which reports does leadership rely on each month, and who prepares them?
  2. Which reports go to the board, lenders or investors?
  3. Which KPIs matter most, and are their definitions written down?
  4. Where do numbers currently disagree between reports or systems?

04Close and data quality

  1. What does your close calendar look like, and on which day are numbers final?
  2. How are FX rates sourced and recorded each month?
  3. How often are new accounts or dimension values created?
  4. Which months are closed and safe to use for reconciliation?

05People and access

  1. Who owns the chart of accounts and approves mapping decisions?
  2. Who can grant read-only, MFA-protected access to each system?
  3. Who will run the process after handover, and how much time can they give?
  4. Are there security or data-residency policies we need to follow?

06Timing

  1. Is there a deadline, such as a board meeting, audit or budget cycle?
  2. Are other system changes planned in the next six months?
  3. What would make this project a success in your words?
